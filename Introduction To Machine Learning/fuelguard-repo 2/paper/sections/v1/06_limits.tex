\section{Limits and what we cannot claim}\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{We cannot claim to beat the market on price.} Next month's diesel cost is not predictable
beyond the futures price; the product reduces \emph{risk}, not the expected cost.
\item \textbf{We cannot claim that machine learning beats a simple rule.} Our 57-feature models have
significant but modest skill and are matched or beaten by implied volatility alone. The honest product is a
parsimonious, volatility-driven risk score; complex models are challengers to monitor.
\item \textbf{Shocks that start from calm markets are not anticipated} (2023, 2026). No model trained on
past prices can foresee a geopolitical or supply shock; the signal reacts after the first move.
\item \textbf{Data and contract mismatch.} We use US diesel futures (NY Harbor ULSD), not ICE Gasoil, and
wholesale prices: excise duties and VAT (more than half of the pump price in Italy) are not hedged, and the
basis between the haulier's supplier price and the futures is ignored. The 1\% hedging margin is an
assumption; results are robust between 0\% and 2\%.
\item \textbf{Small effective sample.} 4\,700 daily rows are only about 220 independent months and 178
backtest decisions; the CVaR rests on the 9 worst months. Differences of one or two percentage points
between rules are within sampling noise.
\item \textbf{Post-hoc elements.} The parsimonious model and the equal-budget diagnostic were added after
seeing the first results (Section~\ref{sec:results}); a fresh out-of-sample period is needed to confirm
them.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{With more time.} (i) Replace HO with ICE Gasoil and a real supplier price series; (ii) learn the
decision rule itself, by choosing the hedge thresholds that minimise a cost--CVaR objective in the
validation folds rather than fixing them ex ante; (iii) predict upside volatility directly (quantile
regression of the premium); (iv) add fundamentals (distillate inventories, refinery outages, freight
indices) and news-based shock indicators; (v) use a sequential bootstrap for the forest
\citep{lopezdeprado2018}.

\section{Conclusion}

FuelGuard turns a weak but real predictive signal into a simple monthly instruction for a small haulier.
Over 2012--2026, out of sample, conditioning the hedge on market risk roughly halves the cost of the worst
months compared with never hedging, at a lower average cost than a fixed hedging policy. The most useful
lesson of the project is a machine-learning one: with a low signal-to-noise target, regularisation,
parsimony and an honest benchmark matter more than model complexity, and the decision rule built on top of a
probability matters as much as the probability itself.
